# Addendum — 2026-09-27: engine corrections and the status of every published number

This addendum records what changed in the engine and in the documentation on 2026-09-27, and which
published numbers that affects. It does not report new results: the re-run needs the licensed HistData
M1 caches and has not been done yet. When it has, its results are appended below as a new dated section
(see [`RERUN_RUNBOOK.md`](../RERUN_RUNBOOK.md)).

**Verdict.** FALSIFIED — no configuration of the MTF-SMC strategy carries an edge that replicates across
instruments — remains this repository's verdict. It was produced on the engine *before* the corrections
below and is pending the re-run.

---

## 1 · Correction (2026-09-27): take-profit targets used swings before they were confirmed

**What was wrong.** `TFView.nearest_opposing_swing` (`mtf_smc/strategy/context.py`) picks the frozen
take-profit level: the nearest opposing higher-timeframe swing. It released a fractal swing at bar `j`
from the close of bar `j`. A swing with half-width `k` is only confirmed once bars `j+1 … j+k` have
closed (`mtf_smc/smc/swings.py`), so the target could use a swing that was not yet knowable: up to 2
bars early for ordinary swings (`swing_lookback = 2`) and up to 5 bars early for major swings
(`major_swing_lookback = 5`; on W1 that is up to five weeks).

**Where it mattered.**
- The grid: every `HTF_level` and `scale_2R_then_HTF` configuration, 28 of the 42, on all five
  instruments. That includes both cells singled out in the docs: `cascade_W1_H4_M15_HTF_level`
  (+2.00 R on XAUUSD) and `cascade_W1_H4_M5_HTF_level` (+1.05 R on GBPJPY).
- L1 walk-forward: the `legacy_smc` hybrid take-profit uses the nearest D1 swing (`k = 2`).
- L3 random-entry nulls for configurations with an HTF target (e.g. `cascade_W1_H4_M1_HTF_level`).

**Fix.** A swing at bar `j` is now usable from the close of bar `j + k`, as `docs/SPEC.md` §2.1 already
required. The size and direction of the effect on the published numbers are not known until the re-run.

## 2 · Correction (2026-09-27): a filled limit order could be cancelled by its own bar's close

**What was wrong.** In `simulate` (`mtf_smc/engine/backtester.py`) the close-based invalidation of a
pending limit order was checked *before* the fill. A bar that traded through the entry and then closed
beyond the invalidation level produced no trade, although the fill happened intrabar, before that close
was known. The dropped trades are those whose fill bar closed against the position, so the bias plausibly
favours the strategy; its size is not known until the re-run. A unit test asserted the old order.

**Where it mattered.** Every setup carries an invalidation level (cascade, direct and `legacy_smc`), so
every grid configuration and the L1 walk-forward; also the strategy side of L3.

**Fix.** The fill is resolved first; the close-based invalidation now cancels only orders the bar did
not touch.

## 3 · Correction (2026-09-27): a stop inside the fill bar was ignored

**What was wrong.** A new position was managed from the bar *after* the fill, so a stop breached on the
fill bar itself was ignored (a fill and a stop breach on one M1 bar followed by a rally was booked as a
take-profit). That is optimistic: the order of the bar's high and low is unknown.

**Fix.** `Position.on_fill_bar` books a stop inside the fill bar's range as a stop-out on that bar
(conservative). Tight M1-LTF stops are the most exposed.

## 5 · Correction (2026-09-27): the stop-out verification was one synthetic trade, quoted three ways

Three figures were given for the post-fix stop-out check of the 2026-06-05 slippage fix: "the median
real stop-out is −1.14 to −1.23 R on all five instruments" (`docs/REPORT_MULTI_ASSET.md` §3.3),
"−1.02 to −1.04 R" (`DESIGN_DECISIONS.md`), and "stop-outs ~ −1.06..−1.09R" for EURUSD (commit
`fc6af2f` message). The only code behind any of them, `scripts/verify_instruments.py`, pushes **one
constructed −1R stop-out per instrument** through the trade state machine; no median over real trades was

## 6 · Correction (2026-09-27): what the random-entry lens (L3) showed

`README.md` and `docs/MERGE_REPORT.md` said the structured entries were "statistically
indistinguishable from random", "inside the random-entry distribution", with nulls run "per instrument".
The source, `docs/REPORT.md` §6, says the opposite: on XAUUSD — the only instrument L3 was run on
(`scripts/run_robustness.py`) — the strategy's per-trade E[R] was *less negative* than both nulls
(+0.142 R and +0.354 R over the bias-matched null for the two configurations reported), an upper bound
because the holding-time match is imperfect, and the strategy stayed net-negative. The wording is now
"better than random entries, but not enough to overcome costs (XAUUSD only)". No percentile or p-value
was ever published; they were only logged to an ignored file. `scripts/run_robustness.py` now writes
them to `output/robustness/random_entry.csv`, which the re-run commits.

## 7 · Correction (2026-09-27): claims without a run, and overstated test coverage

- "Conclusions are robust to the high-slippage-on-stops sensitivity" (`docs/REPORT.md` §8): no such run
  existed (`high_slippage_on_stops` was only unit-tested). The sentence is removed. The run is registered
  as optional in the runbook (`scripts/run_grid.py --stop-slip-mult=10`).
- "Proven zero look-ahead", "paranoid", "truncation-invariance tests on every detector + an end-to-end
  engine test": the truncation tests covered ATR, EMA, swings, FVG and structure; the end-to-end test
  covered one configuration (direct/D1/fixed_3R) and needs the licensed data, so it never ran in CI. Both
  look-ahead paths above were outside that coverage. The wording is removed. Coverage now: the same
  primitives; every `TFView` as-of lookup at many cut points (`tests/test_lookahead_asof.py`); the
  order-fill rules on constructed M1 paths (`tests/test_backtester.py`); an end-to-end prefix-invariance
  test over both entry models, the legacy model and every take-profit mode on a synthetic fixture, in CI
  (`tests/test_engine_prefix_invariance.py`); and the real-data check. The legacy detectors (confluence
  POIs, CB/DB) have no truncation test of their own.
- "141 passing tests, CI-verified": CI ran 132 of the 141; 9 need the licensed cache. Now: 170 tests,
  161 run in CI, 9 need the licensed cache.

## 8 · Correction (2026-09-27): walk-forward inference units

The sign test "21/24 windows negative, p = 0.0001" treated the 24 windows as 24 independent coin flips,
but XAUUSD and EURUSD roll the same 12 calendar periods. `docs/MERGE_REPORT.md` also said the windows
"overlap"; the OOS windows of one instrument do not (step = OOS length), only the IS windows do.
`scripts/run_legacy_walkforward_report.py` now runs the sign test on the 12 calendar periods (both
instruments' trades pooled per period) and headlines the window-block bootstrap CI; the iid trade-level
CI is reported only as the optimistic reference. The period-level p-value is pending the re-run; the
0.0001 is withdrawn.

## 9 · Reproducibility changes

Dependencies are pinned (`requirements.txt`, Python 3.13); `scripts/run_all.py` runs the whole study
from scratch; every result row carries `git_commit`, `code_hash` and `config_hash`, and resumable runs
refuse rows from other engine code; `output/data_manifest.csv` records a content hash of each
instrument's in-sample M1 frame. The derived statistics to commit after the re-run are listed in
`.gitignore` (never raw data). The versions used for the published pre-fix runs were not recorded.
The agent-instruction file `CLAUDE.md` was removed; `docs/PROJECT_BRIEF*.md` and `docs/SPEC.md` still
cite it as binding, and the rules they mean (no look-ahead, costs on every fill, fixed seeds) are
the ones this addendum and the tests state.
